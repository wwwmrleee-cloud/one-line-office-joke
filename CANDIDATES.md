# Candidate lines — One-Line Office Joke Laugh-Off

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) really a one-line office joke;
(2) sharp, surprising punchline, not a mild observation; (3) clear on one
read; (4) fresh, no office cliches; (5) tight wording. Playbook micro-text
standard: a concrete named person/thing/scene + a twist in the last third +
an unanswered question left open + no preaching.

Rejected cliche premises: "this meeting could have been an email", printer
jams, "per my last email", coffee addiction, TGIF/Mondays, synergy /
circle back as the joke itself, pizza-party raises.

## Candidates (all 1–200 chars, single line)

1. "My manager said to 'bring my whole self to work', so I brought my laundry." (74)
2. "The office plant died, so facilities replaced it with a QR code that links to a photo of the plant." (98)
3. "Dave from Accounting has been 'circling back' since 2019; we're pretty sure he's just a calendar invite now." (110)
4. "I updated my Slack status to 'in a meeting' three years ago and nobody has questioned it since." (96)
5. "My out-of-office reply is just 'No.' — and productivity is up 40%." (66)
6. "Facilities installed a suggestion box, then charged us rent for the wall it hangs on." (86)
7. "The all-hands lasted so long that by the end, the intern had vested." (70)

## Simulated head-to-head (judge criteria in order)

- 1 vs 2: both qualify; 1's punchline is sharper — "bring my whole self"
  taken literally is a direct subversion of real corporate-speak, while 2's
  QR-code plant is a tech gag. 1 wins.
- 1 vs 3: 3's "calendar invite" twist is good, but "circling back" is itself
  a cliche phrase (criterion 4). 1 wins.
- 1 vs 4: 4 is clear and fresh, but its punchline is a mild observation
  next to 1's concrete image. 1 wins on criterion 2.
- 1 vs 5: 5 is tight and surprising, but "out-of-office" is a common topic
  and 1's image is more concrete. 1 wins, narrowly.
- 1 vs 6: 6 is absurd and fresh, but "charged rent" lands as an observation
  where 1 lands as a picture. 1 wins.
- 1 vs 7: 7 is very tight ("vested" is a great word), but "the meeting ran
  long" is the single most common office-joke premise (criterion 4). 1 wins.

## Picked

> My manager said to 'bring my whole self to work', so I brought my laundry.

Why: named person (my manager), a real corporate phrase turned literal,
twist in the last third ("so I brought my laundry"), an open loop (what did
HR do?), clear on one read, 74 characters, no cliche premise.
